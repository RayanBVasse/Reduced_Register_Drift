GNA Research · Prototype 2

McAdams & McLean — Narrative Identity

Narrative identity — the internalised life story through which a person integrates past and future into a coherent sense of self — is one of the strongest predictors of wellbeing and personality maturity in the psychological literature. GNA’s Prototype 2 applies McLean’s narrative identity coding framework through a governed, AI-driven elicitation that scales what trained human coders do in the lab.

§ 01 · The problem

§ 01 · The problem this prototype addresses

Coding narrative identity requires trained human coders. GNA scales the same coding at research speed.

McLean’s narrative identity research — particularly the Big Three framework (McLean et al. 2020) — requires trained human coders applying complex rubrics to written or transcribed narratives. This bottleneck limits scale: a lab can study only as many people as it has coders for.

GNA’s Prototype 2 addresses this directly. It elicits narrative identity material conversationally through a governed AI-driven interviewer, then applies McLean’s published Big Three coding rubrics automatically to the elicited text. The result: individual-level narrative identity profiles, at scale, in a single session.

§ 02 · Theoretical foundation

§ 02 · Theoretical foundation

Layer A — The foundational statement

McAdams & McLean (2013): narrative identity is a person’s internalised and evolving life story, integrating the reconstructed past and imagined future to provide life with some degree of unity and purpose. Narrators who find redemptive meanings in suffering and who construct stories featuring personal agency tend to enjoy higher levels of mental health, wellbeing, and maturity. Current Directions in Psychological Science, 22(3), 233–238.

Layer B — McLean’s developmental programme

McLean, Pasupathi & Pals (2007): storytelling is not merely a record of identity — it actively constructs identity. Telling a story to an attentive audience changes how you understand it. The AI-driven conversation is not passive extraction; it participates in the meaning-making process. Personality and Social Psychology Review, 11, 262–278.

McLean & Pasupathi (2011): meaning doesn’t sit in a story waiting to be found; it emerges through the telling and the audience response. Journal of Personality, 79, 135–163.

McLean, Dunlap et al. (2021) — 4-year longitudinal study: 1,520 coded narratives from 176–638 participants. Autobiographical reasoning shows moderate rank-order stability over 4 years; greater for exploratory processing than for self-event connections. This dataset is the proposed validation target for GNA’s coding pipeline.

Camia, McLean & Waters (2024): narrative coherence and autobiographical meaning-making show moderate stability across studies — stable enough to measure reliably in a single session.

Layer C — The Big Three framework (McLean et al. 2020)

Drawing on 2,565 narratives from 855 participants, McLean et al. (2020) identified three empirically validated factors of life narratives:

  • Structural elements: the architecture, details, facts, and chronology of the story.
  • Motivational / affective themes: the intertwined motives, desires, and affective content — the factor most reliably related to wellbeing.
  • Autobiographical reasoning: the self-reflective processes linking the story to self-meaning.

No existing self-report measure comprehensively captures all three Big Three domains (confirmed in 2024 literature). GNA’s AI-driven conversational elicitation is designed to recover all three.

Layer D — Redemption, contamination, agency

McAdams, Reynolds et al. (2001): redemption sequences (negative-to-positive arc) predict psychological wellbeing. Contamination sequences (positive-to-negative arc) predict low wellbeing. Both are detectable in conversational text. Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 27, 474–485.

Layer E — Master narratives

McLean & Syed (2015): master narratives are culturally dominant story templates. People whose stories deviate from master narratives — including migrants, multicultural individuals, diaspora populations — face particular developmental challenges. GNA’s populations include exactly these groups; its elicitation is designed to handle non-Western and non-linear narrative structures. Human Development, 58, 318–349.

Layer F — Computational and NLP precedent

Automated Autobiographical Interview (2025, Behaviour Research Methods): LLaMA-3 fine-tuned to score internal and external narrative details. Establishes technical feasibility of LLM-based autobiographical scoring. GNA extends this to motivational themes and autobiographical reasoning — the Big Three dimensions most relevant to wellbeing that no automated system currently handles.

§ 03 · What we built

§ 03 · What we built

An AI-driven elicitation tool applying McLean’s published coding rubrics at scale.

GNA’s Prototype 2 applies McLean’s Big Three coding system — published in McLean et al. (2020) — to conversationally elicited narrative. It does not introduce new coding constructs. The coding rubrics are McLean’s own.

The elicitation is adapted from McAdams’ Life Story Interview, the standard protocol in narrative identity research for over 30 years. The AI-driven interviewer elicits five types of narrative scene:

  • Turning point: a moment when something important changed about who the narrator is or the direction their life was going.
  • Difficulty scene: a genuinely painful or challenging experience — elicits redemption and contamination scoring.
  • Agency probe: how much the narrator feels like the author of their own story versus someone things happened to.
  • Meaning-making prompt: what the experience means for who the narrator is — elicits autobiographical reasoning.
  • Life chapter synthesis: the through-line connecting different life phases — elicits narrative coherence and integration.

Elicited narratives are then coded using McLean’s Big Three rubrics (agency scale, redemption / contamination binary, autobiographical reasoning depth scale) by a bounded LLM coder with few-shot examples drawn from McLean’s published work. High-confidence scores are accepted; low-confidence cases are flagged for human review.

Total session: 15–25 exchanges, approximately 20 minutes.

§ 04 · Coding rubrics

§ 04 · McLean’s Big Three coding rubrics (applied by GNA)

These rubrics are published in McLean et al. (2020), Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 119(4), 920–944. They are McLean’s instruments, not GNA’s constructs.

Agency — 4-point scale (McAdams / Adler)

  • 0 = Completely powerless; narrator at mercy of external forces; no self-direction.
  • 1 = Mostly passive; some personal role acknowledged but minimal.
  • 2 = Mixed; both external forces and personal agency present.
  • 3 = Clear self-direction; narrator drives outcomes; takes ownership of change.

Redemption — binary (0 / 1)

  • 1 = Present: clear shift from negative/undesired to positive/desired outcome.
  • 0 = Absent: no such shift, or shift is ambiguous.

Contamination — binary (0 / 1)

  • 1 = Present: clear shift from positive/desired to negative/undesired outcome.
  • 0 = Absent.

Autobiographical reasoning — 3-point scale (McLean)

  • 0 = None: story told as pure fact; no connection to self or identity.
  • 1 = Implicit: some suggestion of personal meaning but not stated directly.
  • 2 = Explicit: narrator directly connects the event to who they are or what they have learned.

Exploratory processing — binary (0 / 1) (McLean 2021)

  • 1 = Present: narrator actively explores meaning; considers multiple interpretations.
  • 0 = Absent: meaning stated flatly or not addressed.
§ 05 · Elicitation protocol

§ 05 · The AI-driven elicitation protocol

Five blocks — adapted from McAdams’ Life Story Interview — about 20 minutes total.

  • Block 1 — Orienting: frame the conversation as being about specific moments, not life summaries.
  • Block 2 — Turning point: “Think of a time when something important changed — about who you are, how you see things, or the direction your life was going.”
  • Block 3 — Difficulty scene: “Think of a time that was genuinely difficult. How did it end — did it lead somewhere, or does it feel unresolved?”
  • Block 4 — Agency probe: “How much do you feel like you have been the author of your own story, versus someone things happened to?”
  • Block 5 — Synthesis: “If you had to describe the through-line of your life — something that connects the different chapters — what would it be?”

Each block has AI-driven follow-up probes governed by response content. Maximum two follow-up probes per block.

§ 06 · Validation materials

§ 06 · Validation materials

Gutenberg corpus — 5 books

  • The Autobiography of Benjamin Franklin — high agency, civic narrative structure.
  • Narrative of the Life of Frederick Douglass — complex redemption / contamination, non-Western-template narrative.
  • The Confessions of St. Augustine — canonical contamination-to-redemption arc, explicit autobiographical reasoning.
  • Autobiography of John Stuart Mill — intellectual identity narrative, crisis and recovery structure.
  • Up From Slavery — Booker T. Washington — redemption structure, contrasts with Douglass on the same historical period.

Corpus analysis in progress. Passage selection, coding, and inter-rater reliability results will be reported here on completion.

McLean’s 4-year longitudinal dataset — proposed validation

McLean et al. (2021) produced 1,520 human-coded narratives with ground-truth labels for agency, autobiographical reasoning, and exploratory processing. GNA proposes applying its automated coding pipeline to the same narratives and calculating agreement with McLean’s human coding. This would establish whether automated coding achieves acceptable inter-rater reliability on the Big Three dimensions — making large-scale narrative identity research tractable without trained human coders.

§ 07 · What GNA offers

§ 07 · What GNA offers McLean’s research programme

Automated Big Three coding at scale — removing the coder bottleneck.

  • Automated coding of motivational themes and autobiographical reasoning — the Big Three dimensions no existing automated system handles.
  • Conversationally elicited narrative — richer than written prompt submissions, more ecologically valid than laboratory transcription.
  • The 4-year longitudinal dataset as a concrete validation target — a low-friction collaboration offer requiring no new data collection.
  • Coverage of non-Western and multicultural narrative structures — populations whose stories deviate from master narrative templates and are under-represented in existing narrative identity corpora.
  • Proposed collaboration: GNA applies its coding pipeline to McLean’s existing dataset; inter-rater reliability calculated; results published as an automated coding validation paper with McLean as co-author.
