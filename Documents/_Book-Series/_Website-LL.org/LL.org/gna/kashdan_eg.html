GNA Research · Prototype 1

Barrett & Kashdan — Emotional Granularity

Emotional granularity — the precision with which a person distinguishes and names their emotional states — is one of the strongest known predictors of psychological health, adaptive self-regulation, and resilience. GNA’s Prototype 1 operationalises Kashdan and Barrett’s framework through a governed, AI-driven conversational elicitation that recovers what experience sampling compresses into repeated numerical ratings.

§ 01 · The problem

§ 01 · The problem this prototype addresses

The dominant measurement method requires two weeks of daily self-report. A single governed conversation can recover the same signal.

Emotional granularity research relies on experience sampling (ESM): participants report their emotions multiple times daily over two to four weeks. This is methodologically burdensome, expensive, and excludes large populations — those with demanding schedules, limited time, or low tolerance for repeated interruption — from the research.

No single-session conversational tool exists that can elicit, score, and profile emotional granularity with comparable validity. GNA’s Prototype 1 is built to fill this gap: a governed AI-driven conversation that elicits the same emotional differentiation signal ESM captures over weeks, in a single 15-minute session.

§ 02 · Theoretical foundation

§ 02 · Theoretical foundation

Layer A — The foundational construct

Barrett (2004): emotional granularity first defined as the precision with which individuals recognise, distinguish, and articulate experienced emotions. Low-granularity individuals use vague global terms (‘bad’, ‘upset’); high-granularity individuals use fine-grained categories (‘humiliated’, ‘apprehensive’, ‘wistful’). Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 87, 266–281.

Barrett (2017) — Theory of Constructed Emotion: emotions are not fixed categories waiting to be detected. They are constructed predictively by the brain, drawing on language, concept, and social context. This means emotional granularity is trainable — and that conversation-based elicitation is theoretically coherent as an assessment approach. Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience, 12(1), 1–23.

Layer B — Kashdan’s empirical programme

Kashdan, Barrett & McKnight (2015): people with higher emotional granularity are 20–50% less likely to retaliate aggressively when hurt. They show less neural reactivity to social rejection. They experience less severe anxiety, depression, and eating disorders. Current Directions in Psychological Science, 24(1), 10–16.

Kashdan, Ferssizidis, Collins & Muraven (2010): during a 3-week study with social drinkers, those with higher emotional granularity consumed less alcohol under distress. First direct demonstration of real-world behavioural consequences of low granularity. Psychological Science, 21, 1341–1347.

Kashdan & Rottenberg (2010) — Psychological flexibility: emotional granularity sits within the broader framework of psychological flexibility — the ability to adapt to situational demands. High granularity enables flexible emotional response. Clinical Psychology Review, 30, 865–878.

Kashdan et al. (2020) — Five-Dimensional Curiosity Scale: joyous exploration, deprivation sensitivity, stress tolerance, social curiosity, and thrill-seeking. Curious people explore their emotional states more precisely. GNA’s elicitation includes a curiosity probe.

Layer C — Clinical and health evidence

Sustained replication across two decades establishes that low emotional granularity is associated with depression, anxiety disorders, eating disorders, schizophrenia, autism, and borderline personality disorder. The asymmetry between negative affect granularity (strongly protective) and positive affect granularity (mixed evidence) informs GNA’s question weighting — negative affect probing carries more weight.

Layer D — The NLP bridge

Vishnubhotla et al. (2024, EMNLP): first paper to derive computational measures of emotional granularity from text (social media) rather than self-report. Text-derived granularity scores are significantly lower for people self-reporting mental health conditions versus controls. This establishes the technical precedent for text-based granularity scoring. GNA extends this from passive social media data to governed conversational elicitation. Proceedings of EMNLP 2024, 19168–19185.

§ 03 · What we built

§ 03 · What we built

A governed, AI-driven conversational tool that elicits emotional granularity through Kashdan and Barrett’s own theoretical prompts.

GNA’s Prototype 1 does not introduce new scoring constructs. It operationalises the emotional granularity framework through a governed AI-driven conversation that actively prompts for precision — replicating within a single session the differentiation signal that experience sampling captures over weeks.

The tool uses a tiered emotion vocabulary drawn from published instruments (PANAS, PANAS-X, Plutchik’s Wheel of Emotions, NRC Emotion Lexicon) to score the precision of emotional language in real time. When a participant uses a vague term, the AI-driven interviewer probes for greater specificity. The session produces both a granularity profile and a narrative record of how the participant describes their emotional life.

The elicitation follows five blocks:

  • Opening anchor: current emotional state, completely open-ended — no prompts, no options.
  • Precision probe: triggered automatically when vague terms are used — ‘can you go further than that?’
  • Negative affect block: recent difficulty, whether stress feels the same or varies, emotions that are hard to name.
  • Positive affect block: recent genuinely good moment named precisely; distinctions within positive affect.
  • Curiosity probe: drawing on Kashdan’s Five-Dimensional Curiosity Scale — response to the new and unfamiliar.

Total session: 12–18 exchanges, approximately 15 minutes.

§ 04 · Measurement instruments

§ 04 · Measurement instruments (validation benchmarks)

GNA validates elicited granularity scores against the following published instruments:

PANAS-X (Watson & Clark, 1994): expanded Positive and Negative Affect Schedule — 60 emotion terms across 11 subscales. Provides the tier structure for scoring vocabulary range.

NRC Emotion Lexicon (Mohammad & Turney): freely available lexicon of 14,000+ words mapped to 8 basic emotions and valence/arousal dimensions. Available: saifmohammad.com/WebPages/NRC-Emotion-Lexicon.htm

Five-Dimensional Curiosity Scale — Revised (5DCR, Kashdan et al. 2020): validates the curiosity probe dimension of GNA’s session.

Instrument items are not reproduced here. Full scales available from the cited sources.

§ 05 · Elicitation protocol

§ 05 · The AI-driven elicitation protocol

Five blocks — about 15 minutes total.

  • Block 1 — Opening state: “How are you feeling right now, at this moment? Take as much space as you need.”
  • Block 2 — Precision probe: triggered when vague terms are used — “Can you go further than that — what kind of [word they used] is it?” Maximum 2 follow-up probes per response.
  • Block 3 — Negative affect: recent difficulty described; whether stress feels the same or varies; an emotion that is hard to name.
  • Block 4 — Positive affect: recent genuinely good moment named precisely; distinctions between satisfaction, excitement, relief, joy, awe.
  • Block 5 — Curiosity probe: “When you encounter something new or unfamiliar — a place, an idea, a person — what do you feel?”

The session produces: emotional vocabulary range score, negative granularity sub-score, positive granularity sub-score, and a probe-response improvement rate (did precision increase after the interviewer probed?).

§ 06 · Validation materials

§ 06 · Validation materials

Gutenberg corpus — 5 books

  • The Autobiography of Benjamin Franklin — predominantly factual narrative; low-end anchor.
  • Narrative of the Life of Frederick Douglass — high emotional precision and complexity.
  • The Confessions of St. Augustine — broad affective range across negative and positive states.
  • Autobiography of John Stuart Mill — explicit emotional vocabulary in the mental crisis account.
  • The Story of My Life — Helen Keller — distinctive sensory-emotional vocabulary.

Corpus analysis in progress. Passage selection, scoring, and inter-rater reliability results will be reported here on completion.

Pilot conversations

8–10 pilot conversations run through the AI-driven elicitation tool. Three demonstration reports generated: low granularity profile, medium granularity, high granularity.

§ 07 · What GNA offers

§ 07 · What GNA offers Kashdan and Barrett’s research programme

Single-session equivalent of two weeks of experience sampling.

  • A single-session equivalent of ESM — validated against existing instruments, without the two-week administration burden.
  • Scale: thousands of participants without per-participant daily follow-up.
  • Systematic positive affect granularity coverage — the understudied side of the construct.
  • Longitudinal tracking: the same protocol administered monthly tracks whether granularity changes over time.
  • Proposed collaboration: participants complete the AI-driven elicitation tool and standard ESM instruments; scores are correlated; the finding — whether a single governed session approximates two weeks of sampling — is the publication.
